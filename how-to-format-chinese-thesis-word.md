# How to Format a Chinese Thesis in Word Without Losing a Weekend

If you are writing a thesis in Chinese while studying abroad, you already know the hardest part is not the research. It is the formatting. Your university wants SimSun body text, Heiti headings, 1.5x line spacing, first-line indents, and page numbers in exactly the right place — and Word's built-in styles were not designed for any of that.

Here is the manual way to do it, the rules that matter, and the shortcut if you would rather not do it by hand.

## The formatting rules most Chinese universities ask for

Every university has its own template, so always check yours first. But the pattern below covers the majority of Chinese thesis requirements:

- **Body text:** SimSun （宋体）, 12pt (小四）, 1.5x line spacing, first-line indent of 2 characters.
- **Headings:** Heiti （黑体） — Chapter （第X章） at 16pt, section (1.1) at 14pt, subsection (1.1.1) at 12pt, all bold.
- **Margins:** typically 2.5cm top and bottom, 2cm left and right. Check your template.
- **Page numbers:** bottom center, starting from the first page of the main text (the cover and abstract usually have no number, or use Roman numerals).
- **Captions:** figure and table captions in SimSun, 10.5pt (五号）, centered above tables and below figures.

The most common reason a thesis gets sent back is not the content. It is a heading that is the wrong size, a body paragraph with no first-line indent, or page numbers that restart in the wrong place.

## Doing it by hand in Word

### 1. Set up Styles first, format never again

Do not select text and change fonts by hand. Open the Styles pane, right-click **Normal**, and set SimSun 12pt, 1.5x spacing, first-line indent 2 characters (in Paragraph settings, set Special to "First line" and the value to 2 characters — Word supports character-based indents for Chinese text). Then modify **Heading 1**, **Heading 2**, and **Heading 3** the same way: Heiti, bold, the right sizes, and the spacing before/after each heading your template specifies.

Once your styles are right, every chapter you write inherits them. If you format by hand instead, you will be re-doing it the night before the deadline.

### 2. Use real heading styles, not bigger bold text

Word can only build a table of contents, navigate your document, and number your chapters automatically if your headings use the Heading 1/2/3 styles. Typing a chapter title in big bold text looks the same on screen and breaks everything else: no automatic contents page, no navigation pane, no cross-references.

For Chinese chapter headings (第X章）, use Heading 1 and let Word's multilevel list handle the numbering. Set the numbering format to include the 第 and 章 characters so you never type them by hand.

### 3. Fix page numbers with sections, not manual typing

Insert a section break (not a page break) where the main text starts. Then unlink the footer from the previous section, and set the page number format to start at 1. This is the step most people get wrong: if you just type numbers into the footer, they will be wrong on every page after the first, and they will not update when you add pages.

### 4. Tables and figures last

Finish the text first, then do captions. Word's "Insert Caption" feature numbers figures and tables automatically, and you can cross-reference them ("see Figure 3") so the numbers update when you add a new figure in the middle. Doing captions by hand means renumbering everything after every edit.
